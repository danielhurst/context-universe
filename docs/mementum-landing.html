Results look promising. Then performance flattens once the obvious structured data is consumed. The deeper layer — how decisions actually get made, what constraints can't be violated, what the organisation really believes — stays locked in people's heads and disappears when they leave.

Mementum solves this before it becomes a production crisis. Not another pilot. A compounding intelligence asset that survives staff turnover, model upgrades, and vendor changes.

Section 01

Why AI pilots plateau

The pattern is consistent. A leadership team approves an AI initiative. A platform is selected. A pilot scoped. Initial results impress the steering committee. Then performance flattens. The model confuses recent policy with outdated procedure. A critical decision gets made without the implicit constraint every experienced person knew but nobody had written down.

The conventional diagnosis blames the model, the data quality, or the implementation partner. These are proxies.

The fundamental failure in enterprise AI is not technical. It's architectural. Organisations have never needed to build a system for capturing the knowledge that drives performance — until now.
Anchor Scenario A mid-tier financial services firm engaged an AI partner to accelerate their pricing workflow. The system had access to product documentation, pricing tables, and approval history. Six months in, the project stalled. The AI was technically functional but practically unreliable — recommendations conflicted with risk parameters that had never been written down. The root cause wasn't the model. It was that the firm's pricing logic lived in the heads of three people who'd been doing it for a decade. Nobody had ever built a structured picture of what that reasoning actually was.

The second is a structural advantage. Most organisations have built the first and called it done.

Section 02

The reframe: a knowledge architecture problem

The organisations that move AI from pilot to production share one characteristic: before they built anything, they understood what the AI needed to know and structured it deliberately. This is not a data engineering problem or a prompt engineering problem. It is a knowledge architecture problem.

The Central Insight

Trust in an AI system is not earned by the model. It's earned by the knowledge it's built on. Deep, specific, current knowledge makes a mid-tier model outperform a frontier model running on a shallow base. Model selection is a second-order decision. Knowledge architecture is the first.

The iceberg: what data readiness misses

Conscious vs Unconscious Reasoning

Data gives an AI its conscious reasoning — facts and records it can retrieve and cite. Mementum gives it its unconscious reasoning — the judgment, instincts, and situational awareness that experienced people apply without thinking. One knows the rules. The other knows when to apply them differently.

The eight knowledge categories

Every Context Universe is structured across eight categories. Each has a different capture method, decay rate, and failure mode when absent.

Interpretation
How this organisation reads external signals through its own lens. What the same event means here vs. elsewhere.
Decision & Reasoning
The logic chain behind significant choices — trade-offs weighed, alternatives rejected, principles applied.
Standing Position
Current authorised views on strategy, risk appetite, and market stance. What is true now vs. what was once true.
Real Process
How work actually gets done vs. how it's documented. The workarounds and informal escalation paths.
Observed Pattern
Recurring signals experienced people recognise and use to anticipate, not react.
Implicit Constraint
Boundaries governing decisions that aren't in policy. What would fracture a key relationship.
Relationship Context
History, trust levels, and communication preferences with key clients and counterparties.
Assumption
Beliefs underpinning strategy. Often unexamined. Always consequential when wrong.

Section 04

The Context Universe: a living second brain

The Context Universe is not a knowledge repository. Repositories are static — they accumulate content and decay quietly. The Context Universe is a deployable second brain: a living intelligence layer that is engaged with, interrogated, and updated by the people who know the organisation best. It has an interface. It responds to queries. It compounds with use.

What "living" means

Every decision processed through the system is an opportunity to surface a gap, confirm a constraint, or update a standing position. Every human-approved update makes the next decision more reliable. After twelve months of active use, a well-maintained Context Universe contains more accurate, specific, and current organisational knowledge than any single expert — because it has absorbed the reasoning of many experts, captured edge cases as they occurred, and had standing positions updated in real time.

Stage 1

What happens: One structured hour. One question — what is your GenAI moat? We map current AI position, surface the knowledge gaps limiting performance, identify where the biggest leverage is.

Leave-behind: A simple interactive app defining your North Star — the specific intelligence capability that would create the clearest competitive separation in your market.

Stage 2
Knowledge Discovery
2 weeks · Fixed price

What happens: A two-week sprint mapping the priority knowledge levers aligned to the North Star. We review existing assets, current systems, and real processes against what the target intelligence capability requires. Model the value of closing the gaps.

Leave-behind: A forecasted cost-out and revenue growth projection — grounded in your actual knowledge inventory, not vendor benchmarks. Board-ready.

How the ROI forecast is constructed: The primary value driver is cost removal — the cost of performing work that micro-skills will partially or fully automate. For each candidate skill we identify: the number of skills that can be built in the domain, the fully-loaded cost of performing that task today (people time, technology overhead, third-party products consumed), and the number of times it's performed per year. The forecast is: Σ (skills × unit cost × annual frequency × automation rate). We are deliberately conservative on automation rate at this stage — the forecast is a floor, not a ceiling. Revenue uplift (faster execution, reduced errors, better client outcomes) is modelled separately and layered on top. The number the board sees has a clear methodology behind it that can be interrogated, not a vendor estimate that requires faith.

Leave-behind: A live Agent Universe in the client's environment, priority domain populated, first micro-skills delivering measurable ROI.

This is not the right engagement for every organisation

Qualifying criteria — when Mementum is not the right fit

Mementum is a poor fit — and we will say so in Stage 1 — if any of the following apply:

  • You are looking to buy a commodity vendor product. If the decision has already been made to select a platform and the work is configuration and integration, that is a different engagement. We build bespoke intelligence layers, not implementations of someone else's product.
  • You are not ready to introduce micro-skills to your workforce. The value of this work lands when people use the skills. If there is no appetite or readiness for workforce adoption — whether cultural, structural, or political — the investment in building them is premature.
  • You cannot commit domain expert time during Discover. The knowledge capture in Stage 2 requires access to the people who hold the knowledge: typically 3–6 domain experts for structured elicitation over two weeks. If those people are unavailable or the organisation cannot protect their time, the Context Universe will be built on thin inputs and will underperform accordingly.
  • There is no appetite to curate competitive knowledge. The Context Universe contains your organisation's real decision logic, constraints, and institutional intelligence. It is designed to be accessed by your workforce and agents to enhance their performance. If leadership is not comfortable encoding and making that knowledge accessible internally — even with appropriate governance and access controls — the model does not work. The value is in the knowledge being used, not stored.

These are not objections to work through. They are genuine disqualifiers. An organisation that meets any of these criteria will not see the ROI, and we would rather say that in Stage 1 than discover it in Stage 3.

Section 08

Frequently asked questions

The questions that surface in early conversations — from sceptical CTOs, CDOs who've just completed a data readiness programme, and CEOs deciding whether this is genuinely different from what they've already bought.

DataWe've done data readiness. Doesn't that cover this?

Data readiness asks: is our data fit to process? Knowledge architecture asks: does our AI know how to think? These are different questions. Data readiness cleans the surface layer — structured data, documents, transactional records. The knowledge that actually drives performance in expert organisations — implicit constraints, real decision logic, cultural guardrails — lives below the waterline. No data catalogue has ever captured it because it was never in a dataset. The organisations most motivated to engage Mementum are often those who've completed thorough data readiness programmes and can see clearly it didn't move the needle.

DataHow is this different from RAG over our document library?

RAG retrieves what was written down. The most consequential organisational knowledge was never written down. A RAG system over your policy library will confidently tell you the documented procedure. It won't tell you about the informal check that happens before any recommendation goes to a client — because that check lives in the head of one risk officer and has never appeared in a document. Mementum begins where the document library ends.

MethodHow do you actually capture tacit knowledge?

People can't articulate tacit knowledge in response to "tell me what you know" but they can in response to "walk me through the last time something went wrong and why." We use structured elicitation: decision trace interviews, exception mapping, constraint surfacing, and pattern interviews. Every item captured is classified against the eight categories, assigned a decay rate, and formatted for encoding. It becomes a usable asset, not an archive.

MethodCan culture, guiding principles, and strategy really be encoded?

Yes. Culture and principles aren't abstract — they manifest as specific, observable decision patterns. "We prioritise the long-term relationship over the short-term transaction" becomes a concrete instruction: when a client pushes back on pricing, first ask what they're worried about — don't discount. Strategic intent translates directly into pricing floors, communication tone, and opportunity criteria. Edge cases are the most valuable captures: the situations where standard rules don't apply and experienced judgment is required. Those are exactly what the gap between demo performance and production performance is made of.

ObjectionWe're concerned about sensitive knowledge leaving our environment.

Everything deploys into your own cloud. Mementum holds no keys, retains no copies, and has no ongoing access after engagement unless explicitly requested. More importantly: the risk of knowledge leaving runs in the opposite direction — right now, your most critical knowledge lives in the heads of three to five people per domain. Encoding it doesn't create an exposure. It closes one. Governance controls are built in: role-based access, audit trails, and human-in-the-loop approval so nothing changes without authorisation.

ObjectionOur experts won't want to share what they know.

The frame that works: not "so we don't need you" but "so your expertise outlasts you and can be built on." The most valuable people aren't valuable because they hold information hostage — they're valuable because they make new judgments. Encoding what they know frees them to do more of what only they can do. In Compound, they remain the reviewers and approvers of everything the system learns. Their authority doesn't diminish — it gets institutionalised.

CommercialHow is it priced?

Each stage is scoped and priced independently. Stage 1 is no-charge. Stages 2–4 are fixed price, agreed before work begins — no open-ended day rates. Stage 5 is a variable-scope retainer covering curation, micro-skill development, agent builds, or agent team design as needed. Pricing per stage to be confirmed — contact directly for current terms.

CommercialWe've been in AI pilots for 18 months. How long does this take?

Stage 1 is one hour. Stage 2 is two weeks. Stage 3 is six to eight weeks to a live Agent Universe with working micro-skills. Stage 4 adds two to four weeks. Full arc: three to four months to a governed, client-owned intelligence layer with working agents delivering against the Stage 2 ROI forecast. If you've been in pilots for 18 months without a production system, the problem isn't time or effort — it's that pilots were built without the knowledge layer this process produces.

Elicitation techniques

Decision trace interview: Reconstruct a real decision from start to finish. "Walk me through the last pricing exception that required escalation." Surfaces reasoning, constraints, and informal checks simultaneously.

Pattern interviews: "What do you notice that new people miss?" Surfaces the experienced-eye observations that separate reliable from unreliable outputs.

Signal-to-noise tuning

Not everything that gets captured should stay in the Context Universe. The ingestion pipeline applies a classification step: each candidate knowledge item is scored against relevance (does this affect AI performance in this domain?), confidence (is this clearly true or contested?), and uniqueness (is this already captured elsewhere?). Items below threshold enter a review queue rather than the live graph.

The human-in-the-loop process is the primary quality gate. Volume of approved items is a secondary metric — the goal is a high-signal knowledge graph, not a large one.
